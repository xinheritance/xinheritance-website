About Xinheritance

We build products that feel sharp, useful, and lived-in.

Xinheritance started because we noticed a simple problem: a lot of software works technically, but feels clunky or hard to trust. We focus on making tools that are not just functional, but clear and memorable.

Practicality first We build things that solve real problems, from Shopify plugins to developer tools.
Design + Build We don't separate design from engineering. Quality is better when the same team does both.

We like products with stakes: onboarding that needs fixing, trust that needs earning, and interfaces that should feel better than they currently do.

Our work usually starts when a product is live but feels a bit "off." Maybe the signup flow is confusing, or the interface feels dated. We step in to clean up the friction and make the whole experience feel deliberate.

How we started

We didn't start by chasing trends. We built tools to solve our own frustrations with shared expenses, storefront conversion, and messy developer workflows. If it saves time and reduces stress, it's worth building.

What we believe

Good software isn't an accident. It comes from making a thousand small, disciplined decisions. We believe that clear structure and consistent design are what make a product feel premium and reliable.

Our product method

We build in layers: find the real user tension, simplify the core loop, then deepen the workflow with better defaults and stronger interaction design. That lets us move fast without building noise into the experience.

Where we are going

We're building an ecosystem of tools that work together seamlessly. The goal is to help people move from just "finishing a task" to actually building momentum with software they can rely on.

How we work

Built with intent, measured by impact.

  • Product choices tied to user behavior, not decoration.
  • Frontend execution that preserves design quality in the browser.
  • Systems that stay coherent as the product grows past the first release.

We treat trust like a product feature.

Performance, maintainability, and transparency are not back-office concerns to us. They shape whether users stay, whether teams can ship confidently, and whether a product continues to feel credible after the novelty wears off.

That is why we spend time on structure, language, spacing, responsiveness, and implementation details, not just visual moodboards. The goal is software that feels deliberate all the way through.
